Zoom and pan

Zoom keeps the pixels original quality: when zoomed, the picture comes from the full-resolution frame, and at high zoom you see the real pixels. Zoom is animated around the cursor.

  • Ctrl+wheel, trackpad pinch, Ctrl++ / - (numpad too) — zoom
  • Ctrl+0 — fit to window · Ctrl+1 — actual pixels (100 %)
  • Double-click — toggle fit ↔ 100 % at the cursor · middle-drag, or drag when zoomed — pan (also on a paused frame)

Playback engines

Settings ▸ Playback chooses how frames are produced while playing. Pausing, stepping, saving and analysis always use the exact decoder, so what you save is always the exact frame.

EngineBest for
libmpv (recommended)Media-player smoothness: hardware decoding on the display GPU, frames timed to the display's refresh (59.94 fps on a 60 Hz screen plays evenly), frame-exact seeking. Needs the libmpv library and the GPU renderer.
Real-time pipelineBuilt in: ffmpeg decodes on the GPU in a separate process and scales the picture; used for large videos (≥ 2560×1440) in auto, or always / never.
Exact decoderOpenCV or PyAV (Settings ▸ Decoding). PyAV gives exact timestamps for variable-frame-rate and rotated videos.

libmpv

Getting it is automatic. When libmpv is missing, the app gets it in the background while the built-in engine plays: on Windows the LGPL libmpv-2.dll (about 30 MB), on macOS mpv from conda-forge (a few hundred MB), on Linux it asks once and installs your distribution's package (your system asks for your password). The task indicator in the status bar shows progress and has Cancel; when it is done, libmpv takes over the open video.

Settings ▸ Playback ▸ libmpv… shows the library in use (or exactly why none could be used), how playback uses it, and how to get it on Windows, macOS or Linux. Once per session the app checks that mpv's picture matches the exact decoder's picture of the same frame; on a GPU / driver where it doesn't, mpv's basic renderer is used, and if that doesn't match either, the built-in engine plays. If frames can't follow the display's refresh (vsync off, a slow graphics driver) mpv switches to its own clock so videos still play at the right speed. A file mpv can't open plays with the built-in engine, with mpv's reason in the status bar and the log.

Benchmark decoders for this video (Settings ▸ Playback) measures which hardware chain is fastest on your PC for the current video; the result is remembered.

Playback statistics

Ctrl+Shift+I shows an overlay with the frame rate actually shown, dropped frames by cause and the time spent per frame — handy to check that playback keeps up.

Timecode

Ctrl+Shift+T cycles the display format; Ctrl+Shift+O burns a timecode overlay onto the picture; Ctrl+Shift+C copies the current position.
